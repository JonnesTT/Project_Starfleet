As I seem to find myself in defence of individual trade in [[2. Trade and Crime]], I find myself in the need to explore the motivations that lead to negative externalities in a vacuum; without the coercion of self-sufficiency.

For yes, trade, is one of the currently most abundant systems for the optimisation of the actions of persons in my local cultural sphere. And it unavoidably is at fault for either the whole of, or an increase in the harm done by almost any of the issues facing the western world and it's cultural and economic satellites today.
However, trade is not, in principal, defined by single-minded optimisation. And if it is the optimisation that is destructive, we must then, if we wish to assess the morality of trade, assess whether this optimisation is in itself an outcome of the pursuit of trade. For a perversion of pursuit often comes to define, in the mind of third parties, the pursuit itself. Trade becomes neoliberalism, as sensuality becomes perversion and as militarism becomes warmongering.

Does then, trade in itself, as a pursuit incentivise the hyper-optimisation that is doing so much harm to our earth?


# Extrapolating from GMM Clusters
Using as basis the [Gamer Motivation Model by Quantic Foundry](https://quanticfoundry.com/gamer-motivation-model/) we can derive the behaviour of hypothetical typified user groups who engage with trade for different reasons. Each of these clusters typifies an extreme, and most users have multiple or weaker motivations to engage with their hobbies.

Notably, this assumes that trade in a post-scarcity environment is done as entertainment; that people are not engaging with trade under duress, as almost all members of my cultural circle are. It also assumes that no direct outcome of trade is culturally taught to be an end in itself or that outcomes external to trade are taught to be achievable through it.
I'm saying that none of this is applicable to our current world.

## Action Cluster Traders
The Action Cluster of users is characterised by fast-paced, intense action and high impact moments such as characterised by dramatised scenes of destruction and tropes associated with short periods of high dramatic tension.
Extrapolating from a hobbyist who begins to trade their artisanal creations, I must assume they would seek out highly intense situations. This may include situations in which a threat to life and well-being are present. Situations in which decision making is fast paced such as auctions are also a potential to increase the satisfaction for these traders.
They may therefore fall into a loop of optimisation only in environments which reward such behaviour.
Among forms of trade we consider to be ethical in Germany, these users are drawn to auctions or to negotiate verbal in-person agreements. An extreme user might make more promises than they can keep.
Any form of trade that is not considered to be ethical in their own culture will however appeal to them, as the risk of shunning or socially agreed upon punishment adds to their enjoyment.

## Social Cluster Traders
Social Cluster users are characterised by their enjoyment of comparison to, or cooperation with other people.
The latter is easy to define, should their social environment be aimed at constructive interaction, so will these users.
The former however may form a feedback loop. Should multiple competition users meet to compare against each other, it is likely that they will motivate each in whatever the object of recognition is. If that is personal profitability, I foresee them putting optimisation methods into action which do not benefit the larger social structure they inhabit.
Perhaps social systems of accountability may be used to devalue such through the recognition of harm done or any untoward methods used. It may also help to show ignorance towards success achieved under those conditions. I am not however aware how effective either approach would be.

## Mastery Cluster Traders
Mastery Cluster traders are characterised by their eventual overcoming of finite challenges as well as their maximisation of measurements without known limits.
I see the danger in the former in their willingness to attempt ventures which are unlikely to succeed. They may find themselves in situations in which they feel a need to break promises, push deadlines, underdeliver or reach to taboo methods more frequently than others.
The latter however are innately drawn towards maximisation. Thus, if they are focused on a singular value, they will eventually reach towards taboo methods. They are, however, capable of maximising more than one value at a time or balancing the wish to increase multiple measurables against one another. Thus, if they recognise the value of other measurables, they may maximise a target value while minimising it's effect on these. Or they may even maximise multiple values at a time.
## Achievement Cluster Traders
Achievement Cluster users are characterised by their want to reach finite goals or maximise their ability to affect their own environment.
The former may undertake projects such as establishing a new operation, or changing a failing operation to sustain itself. They may also pick individual goals in quantified measurements to satisfy. They are likely to not get caught in an optimisation loop by themselves, however like Mastery Cluster users they may chose harmful means of reaching their goals or simply fail at achieving them.
The latter however is definitionally drawn to maximisation. The target of maximisation however is effect. Thus, in a world in which trade has a comparatively low ability to affect the social environment -especially in comparison to "soft" power- it becomes unlikely that these users are drawn towards methods that would be viewed as unethical.

## Creativity Cluster Traders
Creativity Cluster users are characterised by their want to explore the possibility space of their environment and assert their individuality.
The latter will be drawn to goals and means which are unconventional. The biggest risk in these opens when profit maximisation or optimisation are societally unconventional, at which point some may assert their individuality by becoming a trader.
The former are likely to attempt trade infrequently, this may move them towards the former of course, but infrequent attempts are unlikely to pose a significant threat.
In the long term, they may also use trade as an instrument to reach other self expression goals. 

## Conclusion
There is some risk for all of these to engage in untoward methods in the mercantile trade, however for most of these the motivations for these are purely instrumental. And instrumental use of trade can allow moderation of individuals by individuals if, and only if the value of currency is conditional upon socially acceptable behaviour. For example, if those who engage in untoward activities are excluded from services regardless of their ability to compensate for them, almost all instrumental use of trade will avoid such activity. Much of the remainder will attempt to minimize the visibility of their acts, which is easiest done by limiting the reach of their negative effects.

Where I see definitional risk is in users who are motivated primarily by Recognition and Mastery who also conceptualise extreme profitability as a challenge. The motivation of recognition can be minimised by recognising negative externalities and untoward as negative, regardless of its profitability. Such recognition would cause these users to be repelled by such activity and consider negative externalities failure.
Mastery users, who are motivated by maximisation, are harder to control. These will always be drawn towards optimising target measurements at the cost of other measurements. But if negative externalities are considered as part of one of these target measurements, they will also be drawn to minimising them. 
This contextualisation will force the innate limits in the growth of individual factors to become visible as these users optimise. One must, however, consider that these limits are the exact challenges that increase the total net value within the economy.

So while maximisation is it's own innate motivation, and that will always include a draw towards producing negative externalities, this motivation can be managed by forcing these users to confront the context of their ventures. And that the following slowing of progress is exactly what notifies others that a challenge is worth tackling.
