As I seem to find myself in defence of individual trade in [[2. Trade and Crime]], I find myself in the need to explore the idea itself.

For yes, trade, is one of the currently most abundant systems of optimisation of the actions of persons in the west. And it unavoidably at fault for either the whole of, or an increase in the harm done by almost any of the issues facing the western world and it's cultural and economic satellites today.

However, trade is not, in principal defined by single-minded optimisation. And if it is the optimisation that is destructive, we must then, if we wish to assess the morality of trade, assess whether this optimisation is in itself an outcome of the pursuit of trade. For a perversion of pursuit often comes to define, in the mind of third parties, the pursuit itself. Trade becomes neoliberalism, as sensuality becomes perversion and as militarist becomes the warmonger.

Does then, trade in itself, as a pursuit include the hyper-optimisation that is doing so much harm to our earth?


# Extrapolating from GMM Clusters
Using as basis the [Gamer Motivation Model by Quantic Foundry](https://quanticfoundry.com/gamer-motivation-model/) we can extrapolate potential extremes from the point at which trade is undertaken.
Notably this assumes that trade in a post-scarcity environment is done as entertainment, in the manner in which other hobbies are.

## Action Cluster Traders
The Action Cluster of users is characterised by fast-paced, intense action and high impact moments such as characterised by dramatised scenes of destruction and tropes associated with short periods of high dramatic tension.
Extrapolating from a hobbyist who begins to trade their artisanal creations, I must assume they would seek out highly intense situations. This may include situations in which a threat to life and well-being are present. Situations in which trade and decision making are fast paced such as auctions are also a potential to increase the satisfaction for these traders.
They may therefore fall into a loop of optimisation only in environments which reward such behaviour. I therefore believe that their pension for capitalism is easy to control.

## Social Cluster Traders
Social Cluster users are characterised by their comparison to, or cooperation with other people.
The latter is easy to define, should their social environment is aimed at constructive interaction, so will these users.
The former however may form a feedback loop. Competition players seek recognition. Thus, should multiple competition users meet to compare against each other, it is likely that they will motivate each in whatever the object of recognition is. If that is personal profitability, I foresee them putting optimisation methods into action which do not benefit the larger social structure they inhabit.
Perhaps social systems of accountability may be used to devalue such with negative recognition or more likely helpful, ignorance towards the success. I am not however aware how effective this approach would be.

## Mastery Cluster Traders
Mastery Cluster traders are characterised by their eventual overcoming of challenges and the approach of them.
The danger in this group may be more socially defined. As studied, they are primarily drawn to games which provide finite challenges, which require effort and delayed gratification. Thus, I believe that this cluster of persons is unlikely to both enter trade at a low throughput model, which is otherwise trivial, and optimise their effort continuously. However, I do believe that they can find enjoyment in the pursuit of goals otherwise believed to be impossible even within trade.
Thus, once put on a path of optimisation, Mastery Cluster users may become extremely productive optimizers purely for the joy of an ever environment that requires continuous betterment of ones understanding of the mercantile craft.

## Achievement Cluster Traders
Achievement Cluster traders are characterised by their will to reach finite goals or maximise their ability to affect their environment.
The former seems trivial. Finite goals, conceptualised through individual enterprise, are unlikely to have large negative effects on the social environment.
The latter however is definitionally drawn to maximisation. The target of maximisation however is effect. Thus, in a world in which trade has a comparatively low ability to affect the social environment -especially in comparison to otherwise allowable social "soft" power- it becomes unlikely that these users are drawn towards maximisation that would be viewed as unethical.

## Creativity Cluster Traders
Creativity Cluster traders are characterised by their will to explore the possibility space of their environment and assert their individuality.
These players are likely to enjoy trade only insofar as it is conceptualised and structured as a way to create freedom and either extend their possibility space or mark them as an other.
I believe that some of these would engage in trade for the sake of trade, once it becomes unusual. But they are not definitionally motivated to maximise their personal profit.

## Conclusion
I see a risk to the social structure only from traders who are either Mastery or Recognition motivated AND conceptualise extreme profitability as a challenge to achieve. 
It is likely that recognition motivated people can easily be discouraged from hyper-optimisation through social messaging that devalues it.
Mastery motivated players are unlikely to be turned away from hyper-optimisation. Indeed, I believe that the willingness to optimise and overcome of these persons is going to play a pivotal role in a future in which their needs are met during the struggle rather than only after. I still don't deem them to be likely to fall towards profit maximisation in such a world, as many struggles will remain even after the end of capitalist coercion.

Therefore, I do not believe that trade in itself is likely to lead to exploitative practices as long as it is not conceptualised in a way which encourages hyper-optimisation in individuals.
