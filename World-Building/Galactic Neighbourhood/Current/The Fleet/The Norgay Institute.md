Named after [Tenzing Norgay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenzing_Norgay), the Norgay Institute is [[The Fleet]]s institution concerned most with mapping and exploring both space and planet. They tend to be the first who are offered missions to undertake expeditions into inhospitable, unexplored or distant places.

# Operations
Most of their data is collected by and from locals. This reliance on local personnel leads to most of the work internal to the institute being focused on collation, translation, and contextualisation of data for use in [[The Fleet]]s public databases. This is the work done on stations and outposts. Though outposts also supply expeditions to almost any landmark considered important to local correspondents, as well as expeditions of exploration.

The few ships they posses have often been gifts by generals after expeditions have already yielded discoveries which are significant to other parts of the fleet. Most of these discoveries are those which inspire further research or invention.
The ships which remain in their fleet then, are often those which are most easily upgraded with new technology and those which are most easily repaired. Unlike transport ships, exploration vessels often resemble patchwork more than design. Their performance and amenities, however, only rarely show that difference.
When given the choice, The Norgay Institute will always request heavy cruisers with expanded internal space. Not only because the extra space makes upgrades easy, if not possible, but also because these ships are almost always capable of full self-sufficiency. With the crews assembled by the institute, these ships can and do function for several centuries and have not rarely spent decades mapping unexplored and entirely unsupplied space.
Due to the tactical advantages provided by these heavy, long range ships, and the enormous cost of constructing them, their recipients are often captains who are known for their sense of duty. Almost all of them require their regular crews to both have military training and be currently enlisted. The expectation that these ships respond to calls for combat support is palpable. During the extensive expeditions undertaken by these ships, however, their captains often seek reassurance in the battle readiness and sense of duty of their crew.

# Notable Customs
A custom within the Norgay Institute is the positioning of external personnel before internal personnel in authors lists and accreditations. This is controversial to many, who received an education in other institutes, but every wing of the Norgay Institute has held onto this honour resolutely since its founding.
