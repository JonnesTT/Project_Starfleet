Disruptors produce a powerful electromagnetic wave which is commonly focused by the inbuilt targeting systems to either affect the whole target or an individual part of its electrical infrastructure.

When the target is a ship or installation,
the resulting wave excites electrons and positrons of the power or control grids to produce erratic power flows and positron conduit containment breakdown. 
Control systems are not rarely affected in a similar manner as they would by EMPs. Potential-Retroflux causes lattice breakdown in diodes, conductors, and resistors are overloaded by sudden spikes in electron flux, and heat generation through eddies in the field damages nearby insulators. 
In most cases, safety latches trigger soon after application, disconnecting each subsystem temporarily from its wider grid to minimise damage. Unfortunately, some damage is always to be expected and replacement parts will have to be replicated.
This damage is also the primary concern when positron conduits are hit, as positrons coast on an electromagnetic field extremes generated for insulation. But the Disruptor impact can also destabilise these fields directly. In either case, the Positron stream destabilises until containment is broken, which releases high doses of gamma radiation as it annihilates together with electrons of the nearby matter. The system detects the unexpected charge and shuts off the conduit automatically, still the internal insulation is likely to have oxidised rapidly and will have to be replaced.
The immediate effect is most likely to be local power failure and a disorientation as systems are rebooted and rerouted.
