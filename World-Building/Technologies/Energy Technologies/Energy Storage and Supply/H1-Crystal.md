H1-Crystals are a form of hydrogen storage that is favoured by the Federation for its extreme stability.
The term generally refers to an ever evolving range of technologies of crystalline structures designed to retain hydrogen atoms unless exposed to specific environmental conditions.

The first H1-Crystal were attempts at maintaining hydrogen in a solid state without active cooling. As such contraptions used to reduce the to a total hydrogen density much too low for practical applications while also requiring constant energy drain.
These first crystals were reliant on the atomic distance between the lattices own atoms. While these crystals retained hydrogen better than pressurised vessels at large scale, they constantly leaked hydrogen even beyond liquid pressure and thus necessitated constant use or frequent venting.
Later crystals were based on a range of phenomena such as piezoelectric crystals, releasing hydrogen faster when a voltage is applied or thermoelastic materials, releasing hydrogen only when heat is applied.
The fundamental idea however never changed, that hydrogen's inherent tendency to percolate through the materials meant to contain it can be used as a containment strategy in itself. 
What also never changed, is that household [[Replicator]]s remain categorically unable to preload them with hydrogen as the force profile necessary simply exceeds their usual needs. 
Most commonly, they are produced by exposing a replicated crystal to the conditions -which otherwise allow release of hydrogen- while pressurising the environment with it.
