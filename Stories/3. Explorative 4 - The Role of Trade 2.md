# Captains Log, Star Date 60082
We are inbound to the Phuix system to pick up a shipment of H1-Crystals for delivery to a system colloquially known as Lesters Last.
While the name Phuix doesn't look of English origin, it apparently is. A small expedition during the 22nd century left a single family of former scientists stranded here. They seem to have had a bit of a humorous track as they soon christened the system Fish choosing to respell the name entirely. Their children, who were later picked up by a prospector looking for hydrogen rich gas giants, cited uncommon spellings as favoured jokes.
The unexpected presence of the unwilling colonist's now known as the Phuix family seems not to have been an issue for the prospecting initiative. Records indicate that the, then adult, children expressed no will to remain in Phuix and the parents would not have been affected during their lifetime.
Now, the system provides several kinds of H1-Crystals produced entirely in Phuix. Thanks to an odd obsession with optimisation by the prospectors, much of this has been accomplished without a need to use what they're selling.

# Replicator Engineers Log, Stardate 60082
We're about to hit the Phuix system, famous for its advanced production network.
What they provide could be incredibly simple. Hydrogen in bottles and crystals. All, including the packaging, could be produced from the hydrogen of Phuix E with a simple industrial replicator installation. One they surely have on their core station. But no! This system was designed by geniuses. 
Slowly and steadily they built a resource gathering and refinement operation so complex that they barely had to tap into their wares. No fusion for energy or elements, at all.
It's said that swarms of drones fly transport throughout the system to deliver heavy elements to refinement stations. Meanwhile, some of the extraction sites still use bucket diggers, modified to be mostly autonomous but bucket diggers! That's 20ce machinery!
I have seen some industrial networks, but I'd have loved to speak to the team who chooses a bucket digger over conventional beam mining. 
Replicators were a thing, fusion all the way through Zinc just became commonplace. Really, there's rather little they wouldn't have been able to replicate. Or maybe that's just it? They simply continued building. Once they had heavier elements sorted out, they just continued making it more and more efficient every day.
I've requested shore leave on the station, just briefly to find out everything I can.

# Chief Engineers Log, Stardate 60083
We just arrived in Phuix, god that name is awful....but the system is beautiful. 
We stationed the ship in orbit on one of Phuix Es moons. I hear the captain wanted the heart-piece of the operation, a station called *manufactura*, to be visible. To this end, he gave orders to orbit here, where we can see the station almost 20 hours each day. And it does seem to pay off. Everyone was in the cafeteria, looking out at the network of exhaust streams from the stations cargo drones. 
It's like, if the stars were a spiderweb drifting in orbit. Parabolic arcs falling around the system, crisscrossing every which way. Each planet connected to the others, even the sun is obscured by a swarm of solar panels. The station itself though, it is the nucleation point from which this snowflake grows. Loading stations around the entire planet send and receive between each other, all streaking large ion trails of haulers back to *manufacura*. Standing low, it is propped up by an unconstrained matter beam. Early 23rd century stuff that, like a fuzzy trunk it simply shoots gas upwards for the orbital station to catch. What drifts off will simply fall back to the planet. Yet still, seeing it in person, this tree much larger than earth with a crown of iron. Countless leaves of solar panels and branches of light reaching all throughout the system. I'm sure this view can be replicated on the holo-deck, but to stand here and know it is real... 

# Ships Envoy Log, Star Date Stardate 60083
The talks with Phuix were oddly short. 
I'm a bit confused with how little they asked for. Any system has some need, whether it's resources, technology, or recognition. Phuix asked only for continued membership with the federations colonialist core.
I understand the wish to ones craft, but this is somewhat suspicious to me. They act like frontiersmen, but materially are industrialists. Like asking for support is admitting to failure, but providing it is an end of some kind.
The notion is faint and based purely on anecdotal personal experience, but if I understand their philosophy correctly it might evolve to become a problem.
As a result, I've chosen to study their sociocultural products over the coming weeks. I'm also going to talk with Dr. Freilich, our counselor in hopes of extrapolating protocols for extreme cases. 

# Captains Log, Star Date 60084
We have arrived in the Lesters Last one day ahead of schedule and are awaiting coordinates for offloading. The engineering team runs a brief experiment of the Warp-Shield core in the meantime.
I consider their political situation to be quite interesting. Having come here following the message of a British explorer, Julian Lester, the colony was ultimately supposed to occupy a single habitat built by this explorer. Their work was to finalize the production chain of a construction servitor. While we have since begun experimenting with such machines, the colonists found nothing of the sort when they arrived roughly a hundred and thirty earth years ago. Most left almost immediately, but about two hundred had already chosen to work on some sort of servitor and so they chose to set down with their ships and retrofit them into habitats. 
As the scholar, Regha Takghis states, "for these however the damage was done, and thus a culture of trustlessness was born." They had first settled in communes, each granting the others only as much trust as they had been able to prove themselves of keeping. Unfortunately, the need to deal with the federation pressed them into producing a centralized node of government. An event we regret to this day. The resulting coalition has proven itself uneasy, requiring active consent from the leader of each of 34 communes for any interaction with the federation.
The Diplomat's core has discussed the fallout of this pressure several times since I joined service. Unfortunately the only consensus they have reached is that the pressure prevented a generation of Lesterites from healing effectively from the betrayal experienced.

# Ships Envoys Log, Stardate 60085
Talks with the Coalition of Lesters Last are ongoing. 
They are... formal. Granted, I am not used to having to deal with 30 representatives at once, but the absurd weight of formality in their political arrangements is, frankly, annoying.
Luckily, I have managed to get them to agree with a process in which requests for support are offered to The Federation without petitioning each of the other colonies for permission first. At least this way I only have to hear how they demand too much only once or twice per request.
I shouldn't be this hard on them, they were raised by the same people whose dream was shattered by the mysterious disappearance not just of a scientist, but their lifes work and habitat in one. They have reason to lack trust, it just reminds me why we worked so hard on ours.

# Positronics Engineers Log, Star Date 60086
I did a random sample test on the H1-Crystals from Phuix... I'll be spelling it Fish from now on.
The crystal lattice is generally well structured with the exception of minor exclusions; holes for the uninitiated. That in itself is a bit odd when the lattice can be replicated with single atom accuracy, but the fact that it's consistently missing samarium does show a pattern.
I don't think these crystals are compromised too heavily for use in stationary, but I have asked Balogh, our Replicatior engineer to take a look at them. If I'm right, he'll officially label them for stationary use only.
I have already petitioned the captain to preemptively requisition a shipment of samarium for Fish. Whether they're cheaping out on fusion or not, we can assure their products are safe to use.

It seems, I have to commend the instincts of our Positronics Engineer Leitis. He noticed an abnormality in the H1-Crystals going to Lesters Last. Granted this did make my job harder these last two days, but he may have prevented a "diplomatic incident".

# First Officers Log, Star Date 60090
We've received an emergency signal from Lesters Last B. 
"I am troubled to request evacuation for my habitat." he said, before explaining: "We can't explain why, but we're currently leaking atmosphere in thirteen locations. All bulkheads are compromised. Even a temporary stay while we figure things out would be appreciated."
Of course the Captain agreed, and offered help from the engineering team, thought the colony representative didn't let us take the pride of repairing it from his own.
We rushed to turn shuttle bay b into an emergency hab unit. And in good time, as a skylight fell directly into their atrium, the caustic atmosphere eradicating a breed of wheat that evolved in almost total isolation for 130 years.
Still, the colonists were determined to save whatever would be left of their home. 
They had left their own envo suits behind, aged things those were; made of metal and replicated rubber. Instead, we supplied them with enfield generators as I accompanied their engineers down with a security team.
These were lucky to live on a rocky planet. Though their belongings were slowly dissolving in a thick acidic atmosphere, their homes still existed. Colourful smoke rose, against the orange sky where the leaks were large enough. They grew quickly. Oddly so, even for this environment. 
Security swept the area, making sure not to disturb any evidence. Only after they disappeared into the thick orangish haze to establish a perimeter did the engineers begin to investigate.
They didn't expect to find small leaks today, but some were evident at first glance. Spots of a tarry sludge speckle the hull, washing out the shapes and colours of their substrate. At a first glance, we suspected that the material was literally dissolved, through what mechanism we did not know. That was until we inspected the fallen skylight.
The sludge was less coherent there, and equally somehow more so. Only the sealant had melted, it's uniform colour flowed psychedelically. 
The readings seemed equally unhelpful, at least in the moment. The protective layer shows holes, hair thin at most, and its material deposited at the edges; not merely pushed aside, but deposited in laminar layers, pushing outwards.
