# Captains Log, Star Date 60068
We have arrived in the Ghlegogh system where we will pick up a shipment of Sub-Plank Wafers.
One cargo module has been reconstructed and loaded at a station in geosynchronous orbit around the birth planet of their culture. 
And a weird case that planet is. With an orbital inclination of almost 40 degrees these people could identify the motion of their planets around their sun long before anyone on earth could. Still, their culture took almost no time to ponder space. Why they delayed space travel until it became economical is something I just cannot understand.
In either case, they are known to be dedicated craftsmen, one of few member worlds to willingly produce Sub-Plank Wafers at an industrial capacity. In return we have agreed to provide a shipment of small fusion power cells.

# Envoys Log, Star Date 60069 (Nice)
The trade went without hitch. In keeping with Ghlegogh custom, both parties inspected the delivery of the other. Tested a random sample and thanked for the trade. 
In keeping with federation traditions the captain offered additional assistance, which was refused, before we made our way towards the drop-off point at the Magellan 17 Shipyard.

I'm frankly not quite sure what to make of the Ghlegoghians. They are very polite and industrial, but every time I have to work with one of them an odd feeling rises in me. As if their politeness is some sort of deception. Still, their track record as a culture has been almost entirely spotless since they learned that offering help without payment is a custom of us. Well, they were the ones to define it as a custom, as they were one of the first cultures we met that did not understand such offers as a courtesy.
I have to assume they feel similar about us either. Considering we offer material support which we are not always willing to provide, they must deem us as oddly shallow. To them, a trade must simply seem more honest than an exchange of gifts.

# Positronics Engineers Log, Stardate 60070
My shore leave at Magellans Port lies ahead and since I got the Booth for that weekend I'll be out there finally selling my custom Holo-programs!
To be quite honest though, I am a bit scared. I've spent months shrinking the emitters down alone. And that's nothing on the time I've spent setting up the routines and decision trees! Hell, some of them are literally modelled after idealised job descriptions. Why hire a bouncer if you can just summon a 6'2 barn of a man?
Unfortunately, how revolutionary these are is unlikely to sell them in itself. That takes even more work still, and I'm not sure I did enough. I'm in the port manifest so I'd assume I'll get a couple of off-station visitors and I have a few display pieces prepared, but this is my first time actually selling. 
At least I have my conversion rates prepared, all calculated down to gaseous hydrogen. Of course I'm open to bartering, not like I'd know the circumstances of everyone on station, but I would hope to get a couple of tons of h2 for it. Chances are good the soft will be cracked and the hard disassembled in due time. Well, I already have the innovations on record, so I guess I'll get the recognition either way.

# Chief Engineers Log, Stardate 60073
We have arrived and are ready to unload the Sub-Plank Wafers at Magellan 17s shipyard.
Seems they got a couple of emergencies in since we've waited all day for handover.
In the meantime we've started ripping out some of the patches related to the Pincoya... field test. We have been allotted a couple of time slots with the heavy duty replicators to replace these structural beams as well as the grav coils on several decks.
To a civilian it might seem weird to replace structural beams because of a couple anchor welds. But I'd rather not hear the shield coil rattle if we're forced to... engage in diplomacy with one of the Pentagonals. Turns out, living ships sometimes size up their competition.

On other notes, I'll be missing my Positronics expert Leitis during these repairs. He's technically more qualified to get the Positron conduits on the new coils, but this is his chance to "field test" the appeal of his personal holograms.
He's been working on the emitters when we started construction and I may have assisted with a couple of the problems, so I really couldn't deny his request for leave. 
I'll make sure to get my share when it works out.

# Positronics Engineers Log, Star Date 60074
Day one at the booth. Bunch of people been by. Most ask what these things are for. A couple though it was cool. Not one bought though.

On positive notes, one guy, introduced himself as Karson, wanted to know how the emitters work. Whether they can project graviton fields too; how detailed or strong they'd be. He liked that they manage a solid surface, the lack of detail didn't seem to bother him though.
Talked for a while to find out what he wanted with that. Was hoping I could add that to the roster, but no luck. Bit suspicious, but it's not like I didn't think about making a "companion model" myself. Bet that would sell like cake.

# Chief Engineers Log, Star Date 60076
We finally got the handover. Dropped the cargo module off near the loading docks. 
They said they were going to shuttle the boxes in via tractor beam. Something about how that new container designed doesn't dock right.
To be franklin, that part doesn't quite sit right with me. I mean, I designed that container. And I did include a standard dock and entry hatch. Being the literal standard. The exact standards that has been limiting cargo tech for 200 years now!
Yea, no, on second thought I cannot believe that they can't accommodate that dock. I'll have a word with the logistics officer first thing tomorrow, find out what's wrong. In the meantime... I think I'll set up a sensor on continuous query to make sure it's still there when we go to unpack.

# Chief Engineers Log, Star Date 60078 Addendum
I can't. I just can't. 
It's about 2300 and the sensor goes off. I rush to confirm from the view screen in my room and of course, the container is pulled off the station by a simple tug. Not even a cargo shuttle, a tug. Like they're waiting with a proper civ hauler just a dock over.
Naturally, I call the Captain to the bridge. Got Tahat, the Chief of Security involved too. Figured she'd know how to deal with that type of situation.
So. We hail the tug. No response. Try to open a channel. Refused the handshake. Which is when Tahat had to stop the Captain from straight up broadcasting a warning. Apparently, these guys tend to picnic when security gets involved. Instead, we make sure the tug knows that we know and tractor it directly.
Thing about tugboats are, they're built to, well, tug ships around. So it slows down but keeps going. It does open a channel though.
Some Andre Karson is on the other side. A little bit panicked he explains that he's from the Karnaz, an independent hauler. Naturally we go to double-check, hold on to the boat though, naturally. The Karnaz is on the station manifest and they are meant to pick up a couple of crates here, not Sub-Plank Wafers though. neither is an Andre Karson isn't listed as their crew.
Now we're calling the Karnaz, pretty sure we have a thief on our hands but having to assume its some sort of misunderstanding.
Now here's where I have to respect the effort.
By the time we finally rattled their quartermaster out of bed, that Karson had reconfigured his tractor beam. At the same time he's pulled us just away enough from the station.
So in a flash, he maxed out the tension of the beam pulling apart my damn container. Just a moment later his new setting sprung up, right in the path of our tractor beam and the engines of that tug brought more acceleration on that little ship than its safety limits would permit. So within a couple of second he was completely obscured by the station.
Now here we are, instructing the night shift to tractor every box individually into the shuttle bay. I'm gonna have to check each of them for damage too. If I get my hands on that Karson, I'll put his cheeks through the kind of devastation reserved for foolish friends.
When it comes to that though, it's not my job. Tahat has notified station authorities and it seems the tug was his own. Is? Might be a was.
In either case, she's gonna handle the security stuff and I'm going back to bed.
