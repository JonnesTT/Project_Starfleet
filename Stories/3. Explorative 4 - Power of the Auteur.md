# Captains Log, Star Date 60082
We are inbound to the Phuix system to recieve a shipment of H1-Crystals. It is to be delivered to a system colloquially known as Lesters Last.
While the name Phuix doesn't look of English origin, it apparently is. A small expedition during the 22nd century left a single family of former scientists stranded here. They seem to have had a bit of a humorous track as they soon christened the system Fish choosing to respell the name entirely. Their children, who were later picked up by a prospector looking for hydrogen rich gas giants, cited uncommon spellings as favoured jokes.
The unexpected presence of unwilling colonist's, now known as the Phuix family, seems not to have been an issue for the prospecting initiative. Records indicate that the, then adult, children expressed no will to remain in Phuix and the parents would not have been affected during their lifetime.
Now, the system provides several kinds of hydrogen storage produced entirely in Phuix.

# Replicator Engineers Log, Stardate 60082
We're about to hit the Phuix system, famous for its advanced production network.
What they provide could be incredibly simple. Hydrogen in bottles and crystals. All, including the packaging, could be produced from the hydrogen of Phuix E usually a simple industrial replicator installation. One they surely have on their core station regardless. But no! This production chain was designed by a bunch of idiot geniuses. 
Slowly and steadily they built a resource gathering and refinement operation so complex that they barely had to tap into their own wares. Almost no fusion for energy or elements required for the last several million tons of hydrogen produced.

Replicators were a thing, fusion all the way through Zinc had just become commonplace when the project was started. Really, there's rather little they wouldn't have been able to build with replicators.
Or maybe that's just it? They simply continued building. Once they had heavier elements sorted out, they just continued making it more and more efficient every day.
I've requested shore leave on the station, just briefly to find out everything I can.

# Lead Engineers Log, Stardate 60083
We just arrived in Phuix, god that name is awful....but the system is beautiful. 
We stationed the ship in orbit on one of Phuix Es moons. I hear the captain wanted the heart-piece of the operation, a station called *manufactura*, to be visible. To this end, he gave orders to orbit here, where the station can be seen for almost 20 hours each day from the Cafeteria. I admit, seems to pay off. Everyone was in the cafeteria, looking out at the network of exhaust streams of the stations cargo drones. 
It's like, if the stars were a spiderweb drifting in orbit. Parabolic arcs falling around the system, crisscrossing every which way. Each planet connected to the others. Even the suns lights is dimmer by a swarm of solar panels. The station itself though, it is the nucleation point from which this snowflake grows. Loading stations around the entire planet send and receive between each other, all streaking large ion trails and matter beams back to *manufacura*. 
Standing low, it is propped up by an unconstrained matter beam. Early 23rd century stuff that, like a fuzzy trunk it simply shoots gas upwards for the orbital station to catch. What drifts off simply falling back into the planet bellow. Yet still, seeing it in person, this tree much larger than earth with a crown of iron. Countless leaves of solar panels and branches of light reaching all throughout the system. I'm sure this view can be replicated on the holo-deck, but to stand here and know that it is real is to know that we, that I, can build great things.

# Ships Envoy Log, Stardate 60083
The talks with Phuix were oddly short. 
I'm a bit confused with how little they asked for. Any system has some need, whether it's resources, technology, or recognition. Phuix on the other hand asked only for continued membership with the federations colonist core.
I understand the wish to ones craft, but this is somewhat suspicious to me. Like they wish to appear as frontiersmen still, but in equal parts be industrialists. As if seeking support was admitting to failure, but providing their product was an end in itself.
The notion is faint and based purely on anecdotal personal experience, but if I understand their philosophy correctly, it might evolve to become a problem.
As a result, I've chosen to study their sociocultural products over the coming weeks. I'm also going to talk with Dr. Freilich, our counselor, in hopes of extrapolating protocols for extreme cases. 

# Captains Log, Star Date 60084
We have arrived in Lesters Last one day ahead of schedule and are awaiting coordinates for offloading. The engineering team is running a brief experiment of the Warp-Shield core in the meantime.
I consider their political situation to be quite interesting. Having come here following the message of a British explorer, Julian Lester, the colony was ultimately supposed to occupy a single habitat built by this explorer. Their work was to finalize the production chain of a construction nanite. While we have since begun experimenting with such machines, the colonists found nothing of the sort when they arrived roughly a hundred and thirty earth years ago. Most of them left almost immediately, but about two hundred had already chosen to work on the infrastructure. These chose to set down with their ships and retrofit them into habitats. 
As the scholar, Regha Takghis states, "for these however the damage was done, and thus a culture of trustlessness was born." They had first settled in communes, each granting the others "only as much trust as they had been able to prove themselves of keeping". Unfortunately, the need to deal with the federation pressed them into producing a centralized node of government. An event we regret to this day. The resulting coalition has proven itself uneasy, requiring active consent from the leader of each of 34 communes for any interaction with the federation.
The Diplomat's core has discussed the fallout of this event several times since I joined service. Unfortunately the only consensus they have reached is that it prevented a generation of Lesterites from healing effectively from the betrayal experienced.

# Ships Envoys Log, Stardate 60085
Talks with the Coalition of Lesters Last are ongoing. 
They are... formal. Granted, I am not used to having to deal with 30 representatives at once, but the absurd weight of formality in their political arrangements is, frankly, annoying.
Luckily, I have managed to get them to agree with a process in which requests for support are offered to The Federation without petitioning each of the other colonies for permission first. At least this way I only have to hear how they demand too much only once or twice per request.
I shouldn't be this hard on them, they were raised by the same people whose dream was shattered by the mysterious disappearance not just of a scientist, but the work of their lives and the habitat in one. They have reason to lack trust. They just remind me why we have worked so hard on ours.

# Positronics Engineers Log, Star Date 60086
I did a random sample test on the H1-Crystals from Phuix... I'll be spelling it Fish from now on.
The crystal lattice is generally well structured with the exception of minor exclusions; holes for the uninitiated. That in itself is a bit odd when the lattice can be replicated with single atom accuracy, but the fact that it's consistently missing samarium does show a pattern.
I don't think these crystals are compromised too heavily for use in stationary, but I have asked Balogh, our Replicatior engineer to take a look at them. If I'm right, he'll officially label them for stationary use only.
I have already petitioned the captain to preemptively requisition a shipment of samarium for Fish. Whether they're cheaping out on fusion or not, we can assure their products are safe to use.

# First Officers Log, Star Date 60090
We received an emergency signal from Lesters Last B. 
"I am troubled to request help in evacuating my habitat." their "Paterfamilias" said, before explaining: "We're currently leaking atmosphere in thirteen locations. All bulkheads are compromised and we don't know how it happened."
Of course, the Captain agreed. And he offered help from the engineering team, thought the "Paterfamilias" didn't let us take the pride that keeping his habitat intact supposedly brings.
We gave them Shuttle Bay B. Originally, we planed to retrofitted Bay A while they waited, but they did insist on doing the work themselves. Meanwhile, we discussed the plan with their Paterfamilias. 
He hesitated, but as they had left behind environmental suits they accepted our offer of lending them enfiend generators, under the condition that we accompany them using the same technology.
I must admit, I don't understand their unwillingness to accept help. But I have found myself liking their eager work-ethic.

By the time we went back down the habs atrium had collapsed. Its skylight fell directly onto crops that had been habituated to the local soil. We saw it erode in the heavy atmosphere, as colourful smoke rose against the orange sky. Hopefully they weren't the only hab producing food. 
They only expected to find large leaks today, but even microscopic ones had bloomed into a tarry iridescent sludge. They speckled the hull, washing out the shapes and colours of its substrate.
The seam around the fallen skylight melted inwards, dripping slowly like tar as we took samples. We scanned a few of the still solid chunks and smaller holes in hopes of understanding just how they were eaten into but handheld scanners found nothing of note.
