# Captains Log, Star Date 60082
We are inbound to the Phuix system to pick up a shipment of H1-Crystals to deliver to a system colloquially known as Lesters Last.
While the name Phuix doesn't look of English origin, it apparently is. A small expedition during the 22nd century left a single family of former scientists stranded here. They seem to have had a bit of a humorous track as they soon christened the system Fish but chose to respell the name entirely. Their children, who were later picked up by a prospector looking for hydrogen rich gas giants, cited uncommon spellings as favoured jokes.
The unexpected presence of the unwilling colonist's known as the Phuix family seems not to have been an issue for the prospecting initiative. Records indicate that the, then adult, children expressed no will to remain in Phuix while the parents would not have been affected during their lifetime.
Now, the system provides several kinds of H1-Crystals produced entirely in Phuix. Thanks to an odd obsession with optimisation by the prospectors, much of this has been accomplished without the need for fusion.

# Replicator Engineers Log, Stardate 60082
We're about to hit the Phuix system, famous for its advanced production network.
What they provide could be incredibly simple. Hydrogen in bottles and crystals. All, including the packaging, could be produced from the hydrogen of Phuix E with a simple industrial replicator installation. One they surely have on their core station. But no! This system was designed by geniuses. Slowly and steadily they built a resource gathering and refinement operation so complex that they barely had to tap into their wares. No fusion for energy or elements, at all.
It's said that swarms of drones fly transport throughout the system to deliver heavy elements to refinement stations. Meanwhile, some of the extraction sites still use bucket diggers, modified to be mostly autonomous but bucket diggers! That's 20ce machinery!
I have seen some industrial networks, but I'd have loved to speak to the team who'd choose a bucket digger in the 23rd century. Replicators were a thing, fusion all the way through Zinc just became commonplace. Really, there's rather little they wouldn't have been able to replicate. Or maybe that's just it? They simply continued building. Once they had heavier elements sorted out, they just continued making it more and more efficient every day.
I've requested shore leave on the station, just briefly to find out everything I can.

# Chief Engineers Log, Stardate 60083
We just arrived in Phuix, god that name, anyway, it is beautiful. 
We stationed the ship in orbit on one of Phuix Es moons. I hear the captain wanted the heart piece of the operation, a station called *manufactura*, to be visible. To this end, he gave orders to orbit this far off, allows us to see the station allmost 20 hours each day. It seems to pay off. Everyone was on the cafeteria, looking out at the network of exhaust streams from the stations cargo drones. It's like, if the stars were a spiderweb in orbit. Parabolic arcs falling around the system, crisscrossing every which way. Each planet connected to the others, even the sun is obscured by a swarm of solar panels. The station itself though, it's like the nucleation point. Loading stations around the entire planet send and receive between each other, all streaking the large ion trails of haulers back to *manufacura*. Standing low, it is propped up by an unconstrained matter beam. Early 23rd century stuff that, like a fuzzy trunk it simply shoots gas upwards for the orbital station to catch. What drifts off will simply fall back to the planet. Yet still, seeing it in person, this tree much larger than earth with a crown of iron, countless leaves of solar panels and branches of light reaching all throughout the system is just deeply awe inspiring.
