# Captains Log, Star Date 60017
I have just taken command of the newly christened USS Dunedin. 
My first officer, Commander Ju Lang on the other hand has served in this role for an extended period of time, the reason for which she has never divulged. I suppose, now that chosen her I may have to inquire officially.

I have additionally requested Dr. Saajid el-Shaer as the ships Envoy and Dr. Tlalli Tecuatl as my Chief Medical officer. 
The Ships Counselor Dr. Rudolf Freilich, as well as Tactical Officer Commander Yuliana Da-Xia have requested this posting. Both of which I have acquiesced due to their outstanding service record.

The Engineering team unfortunately came with the ship, or perhaps fortunately, as my Lead Engineer, Leenane Fuaráin, is also the designer of the ships experimental Warp-Shield core. I must admit I would have preferred a conventional, sectioned shield emitter array due to the downtime necessary to modify the emitter. Fuaráins assurances don't quite put me, personally, at ease. She does admit that this is the specific reason for field tests, but is adamant that the gap will not be exploitable by any known threat.

Including their compliments, a total of 112 persons have been transferred to the Dunedin. Holding 134 quarters for regular crew and visitors, I believe to be well within our tolerances.

Our first mission will be to transport two medical support modules with a crew od approximately 100 head each to the Pincoya system. There they will provide medical support and a neutral ground for peace negotiations between Pincoya Cs native population and their newly unified astroid mining colonies.


# Captains Log, Star Date 60018
Were en route to deliver medical support to the Pincoya system.
While I am not surprised that the Dunedin would be chosen for this transport, I don't quite like the idea it either. Pincoya remains a system at war and we are a transport.

The preparation for this environment will however offer us a reason to get acquainted with this ships experimental feature. Truth is, I do not believe in the efficacy of a combined warp-shield system, despite the practically infinite shield capacity it provides. The engineering team sees this quite different and, truthfully, so does my tactical officer. Both believe that this system can be revolutionary, given, of course, the downtime between shield and warp can be reduced to tactical irrelevance.
To assure that, I have scheduled several brief stops between systems. A choice neither my diplomatic colleagues, nor the medical personnel support for obvious reason. Nevertheless, it is my duty to assure the safety of my ship.

Credit where credit is due, we have noticed that the coordination between bridge crew and engineering leaves something to be desired. The first run was actually a total failure. # Captains Log, Star Date 60025
We have arrived in the Pincoya system. As a precaution, we've exited warp before crossing the systems outer belt and entered the warzone with shields up.
It seems the systems astroid colonies are not particularly well organised as they immediately opened fire. Thankfully, the shields held up well, not dropping beneath a 90% rating during a 30 second engagement. We did not have to fire either, as the situation was able to be disarmed by the Ships Envoy on short notice. A broadcast code to signal our status to the colonies was transmitted afterwards, a bit late by my estimate.

Hopefully setting the medical station up will be less eventful. Module 1, including the political and social saucer was disconnected from the ship today and engineering began reconfiguring it's submodules.

# Chief Engineers Log, Star Date 60026
Construction is going swimmingly. We've had to be a little extra careful and plan construction out a bit more deliberately, but we remain fully in schedule.
The tricky bit is that the station remains inhabited during reconfig. We have to be in close communication with the station for each section. Make sure everyone is where they're supposed to. And of course, to prevent impulse related injuries. Though each module has a local gravity field and active impulse dampening, so I doubt anything will happen either way.

The locals are very helpful so far. A delegation from Pincoya C offered to increase the efficiency of our Photon torpedoes. For now, I've asked for schematics out of courtesy and, naturally, professional curiosity. I know that this ship is not meant to be weaponised. I'll have to get permission for these upgrades from the Captain and Ships Envoy.

# Ship Envoys Log, Star Date 60027
The Pincoya C Envoys have visited again. For the third day in a row. They are much unlike their Belters who seem to understand the concept of diplomatic decency. Though, truth be told I have heard nothing from them.
Historical precedent implies that they don't think much of political allegiance, which would be the correct approach dealing with the federation of the 26th century. Though we too have summarily annihilated our fair share of "terrorists" throughout our history. 
In either case, I have attempted to reach out to them. Unfortunately they seem unwilling to give the location of their leadership up for the time being. A notion that will require in-depth work by one of my colleagues to discourage before a peace can be reached.

# Captains Log, Star Date 60029
I didn't think that we'd have to go on a medevac mission ourselves.
At approximately 0930 localised earth standard time an emergency signal reached us from the far north of the system. After some deliberation and coordination with the engineering team currently on the station I unilaterally decided that we should attempt a response. So we dropped off the second cargo module and went to investigate.
The hope was that they would be able to continue construction using only the stations tractor beam.
To facilitate our timely arrival we redlined our warp drive for less than a second. Credit to Fuaráins design, we reached warp 6 in that time.
Upon arrival, we found an astroid station leaking atmosphere at a rapid pace. Several impact sites were observed and logged. We did not recognise any combat equipment or weaponry among the debris. 
14 life-signs were detected. Thanks to a lack of EM shielding 11 were able to be beamed onboard immediately. 
Ju and a 3 person security force entered to evacuate the remaining persons using personal atmosphere projectors. 
One person was found entirely unaware of the ongoing situation, wondering why the air was suddenly so cold. Another was pinned under debris near one of the impact sites. The last was found with severe radiation burns on their skin surface. Medical has agreed to look into the nature of his injuries as part of the investigation.
We have agreed to provide support establishing a preliminary seal on the station in exchange for their permission to record and investigate the site.
To this purpose three security officers and two junior engineers have beamed aboard the station with Commander Ju.
Based on currently available data I have to assume that the site was attacked using at least two phaser-burst warheads and light phaser fire. The ships warp-shield core has been preconfigured to quickly raise a shield for safety reasons until the patients can be released and our away team recalled.

# First Officers Log, Star Date 60030
We wrapped up our survey of the attack site today. This one is a grim story.
Crewman Juuls had called me over to the impact site he was examining just a moment ago. I was already on edge. Space is eerie even without the dark history of this place. Shadows entirely black, light that blinds. The only sound to reach you the coms chip at your chest. I made my way to him, slowly, carefully, gazing at the radiation burns. I admit it had taken me a moment, but when Juuls spoke the obvious I too connected the dots.
"These burns," he said "are only a skin deep." The warheads could not be, as we thought, phaser based, but were photonic. Technology the Pincoyans themselves had possessed. 
By the depth of the burns, Juuls estimated, the warhead must've been attuned to the absorption spectrum of the material. The grim mark was practically a laser engraved upon this station. 
Juul had already uncovered enough for us to leave. This was an internal affair. Whoever fires directly at civilians is too dangerous to get caught up with. But Juuls has already found more, and I, was eager to listen.
Leading to another impact site, he pointed at the ground. Steel bordering regolith. Nothing special, at least at first glance. It had melted. I grasped for a droplet. Rounded, much unlike the rest of the asteroid. Then he said it: "The drops are small and shallow." Whatever caused this had to adjust the wavelength. Clearly, a sophisticated weapon, made not to destroy, but to kill. Nothing these folks would build themselves.

# Councelor Log, Star Date 60030
I had the sorry task of corroborating Juuls claims by interview. Not all the victims of this attack seemed stable enough to talk about it yet. I assessed the risk minimal to nonexistent for 5 of them, 3 were willing to talk.
To their knowledge the Belter militants had to modify mining equipment and impact prevention systems. 
I cross checked their statements with Fuaráin and the Pincoya C envoys. Seems what the Belters have so far include a disruptor-like modification of continuous tractor beams, near relativistic kinetics, and short ranged laser grids reprogrammed from the impact prevention of their ships. 
As Fuaráin explains, the laser grid would've created a much different pattern. All their other weapons are far from photonic.
That does not seem to hold true for the weapons used by Pincoya Cs operatives however. They independently developed several laser and boson based weapon systems, one of which Fuaráin described as a variable delivery photonic payload. A low powered photon torpedo. Enough radiation to overwhelm the cooling systems of a small ship or fry a person, but not nealy as destructive to larger infrastructure.

# Envoys Log, Star Date 60031
In keeping with the wishes of a faction identifying as part of the Asteroid Miners Union, I have, in consort with the stations leadership, declared an embargo of support to any combatant involved in the civil war between Pincoya C and the Pincoya Asteroid Miners Union.
This embargo has been formalised on the Pincoya Embassy Station and has been sent via a Warp Coaster to nearby stations; arrival confirmation pending. The Dunedin will also carry a copy for confirmation.

Truth be told, I'm a bit terrified of what happened here. 
We were notified that a squadron of four small ships harassed the station, without majour damage, thanks to Fuaráin who raised the station shields.
Immediately upon our arrival Captain Olson broadcast a stand-down. The response took a moment, which he had interpret as indicative of an attempt at attaining leadership approval. Shortly after, we were hailed.
A Captain Krej introduced himself. Part of a militant cell, he explained that he understood the station as active support for Pincoya C whom he called "The Planetary Aristocracy".
This threw uncomfortable reminders of our own history into memory, foreshadowing the incoming attack.
Our attempts at explaining that medical support is extended to both sides of the conflict then prompted the explanation that would ultimately necessitate an embargo. To make Captain Krejs words palatable, the military of Pincoya C is considered more reliant on medical intervention than the militia of the astroid miners.
At this point, our tactical officer, Commander Da-Xia, announced a small object approaching at speed.
Traveling at nearly 100 km/s and headed directly for the station, the impact could've leveled a small continent. Needless to say it would've broken through the stations shield with ease. Captain Krej apologised, but claimed responsibility for the object. It was an asteroid, less than 200 meters in diameter but picked specifically for it's rocky makeup. Krej claimed that he would not be able to decelerate it now and advised rapid evacuation.

I should not commend Captain Olson for this, but the speed and conviction he presented inspires genuine awe. Immediately, he signaled to cut contact and called for Fuaráin, still on the station: "Imminent impact. Astroid. Radius 200m. Solid. Speed 100km/s. Can the shield take it?" I have already heard her reaction retold among the ships personnel, as she seemed completely stunned for a moment. Nonetheless, her advice may have saved our lives.
In keeping with her expertise, the captain positioned the Dunedin 200 meters south of the asteroid trajectory and gave a ship-wide warning to brace for impact. 
The entire ship shook. For a moment the gravity failed as we were flung several kilometers aside. Slowly, systems came back online. "Shield coil failure," Da-Xia announced, "structural damage to the coil mounts. I am glad to announce: We are alive. And so is the station."

I may have gone into details I'm not qualified to record but I would like to commend Captain Olson and Fuaráin for their quick thinking under pressure. I don't believe I could've acted so quickly to save these lives.

# Chief Engineers Log, Star Date 60033
Proud is what I am. Sure, there is damage, but the coil was not designed for straightforward kinetics. Conventional arms are much less directional in their accumulated potential so the coil is insulated but not especially reinforced structurally. No need for that, if it only absorbs vibrations and waveforms.

From the logs it seems that the coil only survived thanks to the square cube law. Thanks to the lower shield potential upon entry, the shield processors were able to distribute the impulse over more distance. 
If I'm right, and I usually am, I might be able to bind the sensors into the loop. Then, if I project the shield directionally, I should be able to double down on this same effect. Play ball at entire fractions of light speed. Goodness, field research is exciting.
Unfortunately, I'm also responsible for repairs that will take no less than two weeks. At least I'm allowed to accept material assistance from the locals. That should speed things up a bit.
